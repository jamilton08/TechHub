Essay checker

    Word swaps

    Overused phrases

    Made-up student · about 300 words
    Sample — “Why are you interested in a paid experience?”

    The first time I fixed something for someone else, it was my grandmother’s tablet. It took me two hours, three tutorial videos, and one long phone call, but when her video call finally connected and she could see my cousins, I understood that technology is about people. That moment is why I chose the Information Technology Support Services pathway, and it is why I am applying for a paid work experience this year.

    Over the past two years, I have built a foundation I am proud of. In my IT class, I imaged and configured 15 laptops for our computer lab and learned to troubleshoot common network problems. Outside of school, I volunteer at my community center’s front desk, where I help seniors reset passwords and print documents. These experiences taught me how to stay calm when something breaks and how to explain technical steps without making anyone feel lost.

    I am interested in a paid experience because I want to be held to a professional standard. When a job pays you, people are counting on you to show up on time, communicate clearly, and finish what you start. That kind of responsibility pushes me to grow faster than I would on my own. A paid position would also let me help out at home and save for my CompTIA A+ exam, so every hour I invest goes straight into my future. I want to learn how a real IT team handles tickets, prioritizes problems, and supports users who are frustrated or in a hurry.

    In return, I will bring reliability, curiosity, and the patience I learned helping my grandmother and the seniors at the community center. My goal is to earn my A+ certification by the end of senior year and study cybersecurity in college. A paid experience would be the bridge between what I have learned in the classroom and the career I am working toward. I am ready to take that step, and I would welcome the chance to prove it.

    Borrow the moves, not the words

    This student is made up. Your essay has to be your story — but you can use the same structure.

    1. ¶1 opens with a moment, not a definition. A real scene pulls the reader in, then it connects to the pathway.
    2. ¶2 proves it with numbers (15 laptops, two years) and says what each experience taught.
    3. ¶3 answers the actual question in its first sentence — and explains why paid matters, not just why “experience” matters.
    4. ¶4 looks forward with a specific goal, calls back to the opening story, and ends with confidence.
    1

    Answer the real question

    The question isn’t “why do you want experience?” It’s why a paid experience. Say it directly in paragraph 3: “I am interested in a paid experience because…”

    Then explain what being paid changes: responsibility, being counted on, professional standards, contributing at home, or saving for a goal.

    2

    Specific beats general

    Instead of…Try something like…
    I like computers.I rebuilt an old desktop with my uncle and got it running again.
    I am a hard worker.I balance school with picking up my brother every afternoon.
    I want to gain experience.I want to learn how a help desk prioritizes tickets.
    It would help me a lot.The pay would help me save for my CompTIA A+ exam.
    3

    Use strong verbs

    Verbs show what you did. Swap “I did,” “I helped with,” and “I worked on” for one of these (only if it’s true):

    builtconfiguredtroubleshotinstalledrepaireddesignedcodedorganizedledtrainedsupportedsolveddocumentedpresentedtestedupgradedmanagedcollaborated
    4

    Sound professional — still sound like you

    Do
    • Complete sentences
    • Capital “I” every time
    • Real names and numbers
    • “I am” over “I’m” (mostly)
    • Your best self on a good day
    Skip
    • Slang or text-speak (gonna, u, lol)
    • Emojis and ALL CAPS
    • Big words you’d never say out loud
    • Someone else’s story
    5

    Skip the clichés

    Readers see these in almost every essay, so they stop noticing them:

    “Ever since I was little…” · “I have always been passionate about…” · “I am a hard worker / team player / people person” · “It would be a dream come true” · “In today’s society…”

    Replace each one with a moment or a fact that proves it.

    6

    One job per paragraph

    ¶1 hook + who you are · ¶2 proof · ¶3 why paid · ¶4 what you bring + your future.

    Start each paragraph with a topic sentence that tells the reader what it’s about, then back it up.

    7

    Same idea, two versions

    BeforeI am interested in a paid experience because I really like computers and I want to get experience and money. I am a hard worker and I am good with technology. I think it would help me a lot in the future.
    AfterI am interested in a paid experience because I want to be held to the same standard as the IT professionals I hope to work beside. When a job pays you, people count on you — and I am ready for that. The pay would also help me save for my CompTIA A+ exam, the next step toward a help-desk career.

    What changed: it answers the question in the first sentence, explains what paid means, names a real goal, and cuts the weak words (really, a lot, good) and the cliché (hard worker).

    8

    Read it out loud

    Your ear catches what your eyes skip. Use ▶ Read it aloud on the Write tab. If you run out of breath, the sentence is too long. If it sounds like a robot, add a real detail. If you wouldn’t say it to an interviewer, rewrite it.

    A real person

    Best tool

    Mr. Cruz, your CTE coordinator, a counselor, a parent, or a classmate. Ask: “What’s my strongest sentence? Where did you get confused?”

    AI tools

    Feedback only

    Ask for feedback (“What’s unclear? Where am I vague?”), not for the essay itself. It has to be your story — and you may be asked about it in an interview.

    Peer review checklist

    Trade essays with a classmate. They answer these about yours:

    1. After reading, can you say in one sentence why they want a paid experience?
    2. What’s the most specific detail? Where could they add a number or name?
    3. Which sentence sounds the most like them? Which sounds the least like them?
    4. Does the last sentence end with confidence?
